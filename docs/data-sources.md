# Data Sources and Provenance

Preserving linguistic integrity requires that every piece of information in the **Tangkhul Language Library** is backed by authenticated provenance.

---

## Provenance Model

Every lexical entry, song stanza, cultural proverb, or historical citation must trace back to an authorized source.

### Provenance Tracking Fields
- **Source Identifier**: A stable ID referring to a record in `data/manifests/` (e.g. `BK-0001`, `SNG-0004`, `DCT-0002`).
- **Citation**: Bibliographic reference, recording metadata, or interview attribution.
- **Section / Page**: Exact locator within the primary document (page number, stanza number, folio).
- **Context Sentence**: The verbatim sentence or clause where the word or usage occurred.
- **Year Recorded**: The publication or field recording year.
- **Permission Status**: Current legal or community authorization tier.

---

## Eligible Source Categories

1. **Historical Publications**: Early grammatical sketches, primers, and dictionaries in the public domain (e.g., Rev. William Pettigrew's 1918 *Tangkhul Naga Grammar and Dictionary*, Linguistic Survey of India Vol. III Part III).
2. **Community Publications**: School textbooks, literacy readers, and songbooks published by Tangkhul literature committees or educational institutions, subject to explicit permission.
3. **Oral Heritage & Fieldwork**: Transcriptions of traditional elder narratives, folk songs (*Luita/Luira*, *Chumpha* songs), and customary proverbs collected with informed consent.
4. **Scholarly & Lexicographical Works**: Research papers, academic dissertations, and comparative Tibeto-Burman studies documenting Tangkhul phonology, syntax, and morphology.

---

## Provenance Integrity Policy

- **No Synthetic Records**: Fabricated entries or machine-generated translations must never be presented as authentic Tangkhul language data.
- **Multi-Source Evidence**: When multiple sources attest a word, each source citation is appended to the entry's provenance history.
