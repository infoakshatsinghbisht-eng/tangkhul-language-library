ETHNOGRAPHIC OVERVIEW & NOTES:
  Foundational sociological and anthropological study of Tangkhul Naga social structure. Clarifies the complete absence of a caste system, examining instead the democratic patrilineal clan (Shang / Shangnao) structure, strict clan exogamy, village governance (Awunga and Hangva council), and youth dormitory institutions (Longshim).

CULTURAL TEXT, INVOCATIONS, PROCEDURES & SOCIOLOGICAL DATA:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[SOCIOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK]
  Caste Clarification: The Tangkhul Naga people do NOT have a caste (varna or jati) system. Tangkhul society is fundamentally egalitarian, democratic, and non-hierarchical with respect to hereditary servitude. Social organization is instead governed by ancient patrilineal clans (Shang or Shangnao), clan exogamy, and village council democracy.
  Kinship Principles:
    - Patrilineal Descent: Clan membership and ancestral land inheritance pass through the male lineage.
    - Strict Clan Exogamy (Shang wui Shimlut): Marriage between individuals of the same clan (Shang) is strictly forbidden. A breach of this law is termed 'Masi-kongra' (spiritual pollution requiring heavy customary purification and possible exile).
    - Complementary Clan Alliances: Matrimonial unions create binding socio-economic covenants between complementary clans across and within villages.
    - Egalitarian Wealth Redistribution: Wealthy villagers do not oppress poorer members; through the 'Maran Kasa' (Feast of Merit), wealth is communally consumed and redistributed, earning social honor rather than coercive dominion.

[MAJOR PATRILINEAL CLANS (SHANGNAO)]

  * Clan Name:     Shimray / Shimray-nao
    Regions:       Hunphun (Ukhrul), Chingjaroi, Paorei, Phungcham
    Historic Role: One of the most ancient and prominent patrician lineages; historically provides hereditary chiefs (Awunga) and supreme judicial arbiters; renowned custodians of oral covenants.
    Insignia:      Hornbill crest (Langva) and royal spear insignia.

  * Clan Name:     Luikham
    Regions:       Hunphun, Nungbi, Tolloi, Ngaimu
    Historic Role: Venerable agricultural and political lineage; historically entrusted with presiding over the inaugural sod-turning rites and boundary demarcation.
    Insignia:      Terraced sheaf motif and sacred bronze gong.

  * Clan Name:     Kasomwoshi
    Regions:       Hunphun, Somdal, Huishu, Shirui
    Historic Role: Celebrated custodians of philosophical jurisprudence, customary proverbs (Chanjam / Paotam), diplomatic arbitration, and literary history.
    Insignia:      Carved council post and wisdom staff.

  * Clan Name:     Zimik
    Regions:       Northern Tangkhul (Raphei), Hunphun, Kharasom, Jessami borders
    Historic Role: Prominent mountain lineage; renowned for high bravery, customary water irrigation covenants, and elder council leadership.
    Insignia:      Mountain eagle and water spring symbol.

  * Clan Name:     Vashum
    Regions:       Hunphun, Teinem, Somdal, Lambui
    Historic Role: Celebrated lineage renowned for warrior prowess, defense strategy, master woodcraft, and hunting ceremonial rites.
    Insignia:      Crossed battle spears and wild boar tusks.

  * Clan Name:     Shaiza
    Regions:       Hunphun (Ukhrul), Somdal, Choithar
    Historic Role: Highly influential patrician clan; has produced illustrious statesmen, writers, educators, political leaders, and cultural chroniclers.
    Insignia:      Laurel wreath and ceremonial Haora weave.

  * Clan Name:     Ruivah
    Regions:       Somdal, Western Tangkhul (Remva), Phadang
    Historic Role: Eminent lineage renowned for scholarship, customary law codification, pioneering education, and village arbitration.
    Insignia:      Scroll and sunburst motif.

  * Clan Name:     Shingnaisui
    Regions:       Hunphun, Lunghar, Sihai
    Historic Role: Ancient clan renowned for religious integrity, peace pacts (Phaikham), and inter-village diplomatic embassies.
    Insignia:      Peace branch and white quartz emblem.

  * Clan Name:     Sareo
    Regions:       Eastern and Southern ranges (Kamo, Somra)
    Historic Role: Respected lineage renowned for agricultural terrace mastery, hunting rites, and communal stewardship.
    Insignia:      Terrace crescent and quiver.

  * Clan Name:     A'Shao (AS)
    Regions:       Hunphun ancestral village site
    Historic Role: Foundational clan lineage with deep genealogical roots; custodians of original settlement lore and village hearth rights.
    Insignia:      Ancient stone hearth and horn post.

  * Clan Name:     Other Prominent Lineages
    Regions:       Widespread across all Tangkhul valleys
    Historic Role: Includes Kashung, Phungshok, Raleng, Kengoo, Mahongnao, Kaping, Muivah, Khangrah, Rungsung, Hongray, Chahong—each preserving specific village roles, clan lands, and hereditary seats in their local Hangva councils.
    Insignia:      Distinctive clan weaving designs and ancestral heirlooms.

[VILLAGE GOVERNANCE INSTITUTIONS]

  >> Awunga (Hereditary Village King / Chief)
     Jurisdiction: Supreme titular head of the village sovereign territory.
     Functions:    Inaugurates annual festivals (Luira sod-turning); leads war defense in ancient times; acts as chief justice in conjunction with the Hangva council; maintains village boundaries with neighboring polities.

  >> Hangva (Council of Village Clan Elders)
     Jurisdiction: The supreme legislative, judicial, and executive assembly of the village.
     Functions:    Composed of representative heads of each patrilineal clan (Shang); deliberates on property disputes, theft, customary restitution, divorce cases, and public infrastructure works.

  >> Pikha / Khunbu (Sacred Priest & Land Custodian)
     Jurisdiction: Spiritual rites and ancient sacrificial custody.
     Functions:    Descendant of the foundational village settler; performs invocations at sacred groves; sanctifies boundaries; maintains oral knowledge of sacred spots.

  >> Yarnao (Peer-Group Labor Cooperatives)
     Jurisdiction: Youth and adult mutual-aid agricultural teams.
     Functions:    Rotates intensive labor across member terraced paddy fields during planting, weeding, and harvesting; enforces social solidarity and youth discipline.

  >> Longshim (Youth Dormitory Institution)
     Jurisdiction: Central educational, military, and cultural institution.
     Functions:    Divided into Mayarshim (boys dormitory) and Ngashim (girls dormitory). Served as indigenous universities teaching tribal history, etiquette, weaving, woodcraft, songcraft, and civic defense.
