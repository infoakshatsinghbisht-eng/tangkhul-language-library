PREFACE & TRANSLATION HISTORICAL CONTEXT
Rabindranath Tagore's devotional lyrics (Gitanjali) found a naturally
receptive medium in the Tangkhul lyric poetic tradition (Hao-laa). The
translator rendered Tagore's yearning for the Infinite using intimate
metaphors of mountain dawns, rain-bearing clouds, and traditional flute
melodies.

CHAPTER 1: GITANJALI SONG 1: THOU HAST MADE ME ENDLESS (NA ILI MACHAMTUI SAKAHAISAYE)
Summary: Tagore's opening offering: the human soul as a frail flute through which God bre...
Tangkhul Text:
Shai-laa 1: Na ili machamtui sakahaisaye, chichi Nawui ningkazangsai. Hi
kacham kaphunga vessel hi Na khani-kathum khangareilaga kupsang-miye,
kala zingsho zingsho ringkapha kathara eina kashung-sake. Hi kakat
bamboo reed flute hi Na kaphung-longkhor kuppupli phungzatlaga, Awui
ngalung eina thot pupupwui shai-laaring honsangserie. Nawui kathara
khutwui katom eina iwui kacham lung china khangayeiwui mathatli
ngapaoritlaga khongyui kapei honsange.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Thou hast made me endless, such is thy pleasure. This frail vessel thou
emptiest again and again, and fillest it ever with fresh life. This
little flute of a reed thou hast carried over hills and dales, and hast
breathed through it melodies eternally new. At the immortal touch of thy
hands my little heart loses its limits in joy and gives birth to
utterance ineffable.

CHAPTER 1 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * machamtui (adj): endless, immortal, boundlessly enduring
    Ex: Machamtui sakahaisaye.
  * bamboo reed flute (noun): Siphung / Pung (bamboo flute of the hills)
    Ex: Kakat flute hi.
  * katom (noun): gentle touch, caress of divine hand
    Ex: Khutwui katom eina.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Metaphorical transposition: Tagore's reed flute rendered as traditional Tangkhul 'siphung'.
  - Verbal frequentative: 'khani-kathum khangareilaga' (emptying repeatedly again and again).

CHAPTER 2: WHERE THE MIND IS WITHOUT FEAR (MANGMATHA MATHIKHAVAI RAM)
Summary: Tagore's universal prayer for freedom, truth, and unconstrained intellectual dig...
Tangkhul Text:
Shai-laa 35: Kachikhali ningkakhaning malei-mana, kala kakhutli phashan
eina thuk-kashok-e; Kachikhali theikakhui chi mikumo pupupwui vang
kacham eina ngashankahaye; Kachikhali ngalei hi shimwui wall kakat eina
kashit-mashit-mana; Kachikhali tui-ngashungna lungwui kashikli eina
shokhunge; Kachikhali khangachonna khut machamla phāmeikhavai otli
thukzat-e; Kachikhali mashung-ngalungwui kongkhamathān china kasham
custom-wui miram kashilashili mangapaorit-mana; Chithuili, I-Ava, Nawui
khut eina iwui ramli ngakhur-rarsaklu!
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Where the mind is without fear and the head is held high; Where
knowledge is free; Where the world has not been broken up into fragments
by narrow domestic walls; Where words come out from the depth of truth;
Where tireless striving stretches its arms towards perfection; Where the
clear stream of reason has not lost its way into the dreary desert sand
of dead habit; Into that heaven of freedom, my Father, let my country
awake.

CHAPTER 2 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * ningkakhaning (noun): fear, dread of mind, timidity
    Ex: Ningkakhaning malei.
  * theikakhui (noun): knowledge, enlightenment, wisdom
    Ex: Theikakhui kacham.
  * kongkhamathān (noun): clear crystal stream of reason
    Ex: Mashung-ngalungwui kongkhamathān.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Locative anaphora: 'Kachikhali... Kachikhali...' (Where... Where...).
  - Aspiration invocation: 'I-Ava, Nawui khut eina... ngakhur-rarsaklu!' (Father, let my nation awake!).

CHAPTER 3: LEAVE THIS CHANTING AND SINGING (GITANJALI SONG 11)
Summary: Finding God not in closed temples, but with the tiller of the soil and pathmaker...
Tangkhul Text:
Shai-laa 11: Hi luishat-laa kala rosary chinawui khangayei kashikahailu!
Shimkhur ngachingli khong kakat khangakhakli Na khipali reot-kasa? Nawui
mik hanglu, kala theilu, Nawui Varivara chithuili maleimana! A tilling
kasana luiramli ot salakha Awui ngalemli lei; A lungkhor khakka-sana
shongfa shomlakha Awui ngalemli lei; A zingsho zingkhamathān kala
kazingkhamik tharili athum eina li-leie, Awui chon ngaleiwui kachak eina
kashung-saye. Nawui kathara chon kashikahailaga, A thada ngaleili varlu!
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Leave this chanting and singing and telling of beads! Whom dost thou
worship in this lonely dark corner of a temple with doors all shut? Open
thine eyes and see thy God is not before thee! He is there where the
tiller is tilling the hard ground and where the pathmaker is breaking
stones. He is with them in sun and in shower, and his garment is covered
with dust. Put off thy holy mantle and even like him go down on the
dusty soil!

CHAPTER 3 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * luiramli ot (noun): tilling the terraced paddy fields
    Ex: Luiramli ot salakha.
  * lungkhor khakka-sa (noun): stone breaker, path maker
    Ex: Lungkhor khakka-sana.
  * kazingkhamik (noun): monsoon rain downpour
    Ex: Zingkhamathān kala kazingkhamik.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Agrarian elevation of labor: God resides with field tillers ('luiramli ot').
  - Imperative detachment: 'kashikahailu' (leave aside, abandon ritualism).
