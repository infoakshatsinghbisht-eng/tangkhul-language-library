## BK-ARCH-003 -- Preface & Archival Imprint
### Preface & Archival Documentation
PREFACE & ARCHIVAL INTRODUCTION:
Published in London under the patronage of the Government of Eastern Bengal and
Assam by Macmillan and Co. in 1911. T. C. Hodson, former Assistant Political Agent in
Manipur, provides the earliest comprehensive ethnographic and linguistic survey of
the Tangkhul Nagas of Manipur, documenting village organization, traditional law,
linguistic relationships, folktales, ballads, and headmanship institutions.

## Chapter 1: Habitat & Tribal Divisions (Page 4)
### Chapter 1: Habitat, Geography & Tribal Divisions
CHAPTER 1: HABITAT, GEOGRAPHY & TRIBAL DIVISIONS
Summary: The mountain territory of the Tangkhuls and four territorial subdivisions.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Tangkhul miyur hi Manipur wui kaphung zanshorli pamda kaphung khangacheo eina ngashitna.
Territorial groups: Raphei (Northern Tangkhuls), Kathur (Central Tangkhuls around Ukhrul and Hungpung),
Kamo (Southern Tangkhuls bordering Maring territory), and Kharao (Western Tangkhuls facing the valley).
Each area possesses its distinctive sub-dialect, while Ukhrul serves as the recognized lingua franca.

## Chapter 2: Tangkhul Linguistic Classification (Page 6)
### Chapter 2: Linguistic Classification & Dialects
CHAPTER 2: LINGUISTIC CLASSIFICATION & DIALECTS
Summary: Phonetic features, tone patterns, and relations with the Tibeto-Burman family.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Tangkhul language exhibits agglutinative morphology with dominant prefix ka- forming adjectives and infinitives.
Case relationships are indicated by postpositional affixes: -na (agentive), -li (dative/locative), -wui (genitive).
Vowel length contrasts: 'sa' (to do) vs 'sā' (animal/flesh); 'mik' (eye) vs 'mei' (fire).

## Chapter 2: Grammatical Notes (Page 7)
### Chapter 2 Grammatical Form & Notes
CHAPTER 2 STUDY GUIDE & GRAMMATICAL NOTES
GRAMMAR POINTS:
  - Postpositional clitics mark syntactic relations.
  - Adjectival roots take ka- prefix: ka-pha (good), ka-thei (seen), ka-thum (three).
  - Negation is framed using circumfix ma-...-mana: ma-thei-mana (does not see).

## Chapter 3: Village Organization & Awunga (Page 8)
### Chapter 3: Village Organization & The Awunga
CHAPTER 3: VILLAGE ORGANIZATION & THE AWUNGA
Summary: The hereditary institution of the village chief (Awunga) and the council of elders (Hangva).

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Khaung kachida Awunga akha leiyi; Awunga hi shimkhurwui mathun eina ngashitna.
The Tangkhul village is an independent democratic unit. The chief, Awunga, rules in conjunction with
the village council called Hangva, composed of representatives from each clan (Shangnao).
He leads the agricultural festivals (Luira, Chumphut) and acts as the chief custodian of village lands.

## Chapter 4: Traditional Laws & Feasts of Merit (Page 10)
### Chapter 4: Traditional Laws & Feasts of Merit
CHAPTER 4: TRADITIONAL LAWS, LAND TENURE & FEASTS OF MERIT
Summary: Maran (Feasts of Merit), Tarung wooden monuments, and customary tenure.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Maran kashak hi Tangkhul ngashanli khamataiya otsak akhana. Tarung thingrei vakhang eina shimli shona.
Feasts of Merit (Maran) elevate the social and religious standing of a family in this world and the next.
The celebrant pulls a colossal fork-shaped wooden monument (Tarung) from the forest, accompanied by whole-village singing.

## Chapter 5: Folktales: Origin of Tangkhuls (Page 12)
### Chapter 5: Tangkhul Folktales: Origins
CHAPTER 5: TANGKHUL FOLKTALES: ORIGIN OF THE SPECIES & ANIMALS
Summary: The legendary migration from Samsok and brotherhood between the Meitei and Tangkhul.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Awo-ayiwui khararchanli: 'Avah Samsokli tungthuiya, Makungana ili phungtung.'
Tangkhul folklore recounts that the elder brother remained in the eastern hills to hunt and clear terrace fields,
while the younger brother descended to cultivate the fertile valley of Manipur, establishing the historical
brotherhood bond commemorated at the annual Mera Hou Chongba festival.

## Chapter 6: Traditional Tangkhul Ballads (Page 14)
### Chapter 6: Traditional Tangkhul Ballads & Folk Poetry
CHAPTER 6: TRADITIONAL TANGKHUL BALLADS & FOLK POETRY
Summary: The tragic romance of Shimreishang and Maitonphi, and traditional war ballads.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Shimreishang eina Maitonphiwui leikashiyi Tangkhul thotchanli kaphapamei laana.
'Avayo avayo, makhamalu Maitonphili rasha saka; sakhangaiye Maitonphi kharam thuihaowa.'
The lyrical lament describes immortal love that defies village taboos and reunites in Kazeiram (the afterlife).

## Chapter 7: Dialectal Comparison (Page 18)
### Chapter 7 Dialectal Comparison Notes
CHAPTER 7 (CONTINUED): DIALECTAL COMPARATIVE MATRIX
Ukhrul (Kathur): tara (water), mei (fire), si (dog), mi (man).
Raphei (Northern): ra (water), mai (fire), shei (dog), rimi (man).
Kamo (Southern): ti (water), mee (fire), thii (dog), mi (man).
Phonetic correspondence shows consistent tonal and glide shifts between dialects.
