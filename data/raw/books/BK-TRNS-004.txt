PREFACE & TRANSLATION HISTORICAL CONTEXT
The Book of Genesis (Avara Kasa wui Khararchan) establishes the
foundational cosmogony of the Tangkhul Bible. Translated and refined
across successive generations by native scholars and the Bible Society
of India, it bridges ancient Semitic narratives with the rich ecological
and agrarian vocabulary of the Naga hills.
Archival Catalog Reference: BSI-TKH-GEN-1965

CHAPTER 1: THE CREATION OF THE WORLD (GENESIS 1)
Summary: The six days of creation and the Sabbath rest in grand ceremonial Tangkhul dicti...
Tangkhul Text:
Genesis 1:1-5, 26-27: Kaphung-kachungwui thotli Varivarana kazing kala
ngalei sakahaisai. Kala ngalei chi kasham eina khangari-leilakha leisai,
kala ngaching china kapei ngashakazilli chumpamsai; kala Varivarawui
Mangazingna tui-ngapomli ngazuklaga pamye. Chitharan Varivarana hang:
'Mangashan shokru!' Kala mangashan shokserie. Kala Varivarana mangashan
kaphā chi theitserie, kala mangashan kala ngachingli kashit-eina
pamsakserie. ...Kala Varivarana hang: 'Ithuwui mai thada, ithuwui katha
eina mikumoli saru; athumna ngashakazilwui ngachayei, kazingramwui
vanao, kala ngaleiwui saphang kuppupli ngashankasarap sara.' Hithada
Varivarana Awui mai thada mikumoli sakahaisai; mayarnaowon kala
ngalanaowon eina sakahaisai.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. And the earth was
without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And
the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. And God said, Let
there be light: and there was light. And God saw the light, that it was
good: and God divided the light from the darkness. ...And God said, Let
us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion
over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the
cattle, and over all the earth. So God created man in his own image, in
the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.

CHAPTER 1 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * sakahaisai (verb): created / made definitively
    Ex: Varivarana sakahaisai.
  * ngazuklaga (verb): hovering, brooding over gently
    Ex: Tui-ngapomli ngazuklaga.
  * saphang (noun): beasts of the earth, animal kingdom
    Ex: Ngaleiwui saphang.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Divine fiat imperative formula: 'shokru!' (Let there be!).
  - Gender pair nominalization: 'mayarnaowon kala ngalanaowon' (male and female).

CHAPTER 2: THE GARDEN OF EDEN & THE TREE OF LIFE (GENESIS 2-3)
Summary: The planting of Eden, the two sacred trees, and the covenant of moral responsibi...
Tangkhul Text:
Genesis 2:8-9, 3:1-6: Kala Proho Varivarana kashong-zingsho eina Edenli
luiram akha shansangie; kala Ana sakahai mikumoli chithuili pamsakserie.
Kala Proho Varivarana ngalei eina thingphun kathata mathāthei-kaphā kala
kashak-kaphā pamei phokrarsakserie; luiram ngalungli ringkaphavai
thingphun kala kaphā-kashili theikhavai thingphun leisai. ...Kha phar
china Proho Varivarana sakahai ngaleiwui saphang kuppup eina chashak-
kaphā pameisai; ana shanaoli hang: 'Varivarana luiramwui thingthei
kachikhali mashak-alu hoda hung-kachak lei-macho?'
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
And the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden; and there he put the
man whom he had formed. And out of the ground made the LORD God to grow
every tree that is pleasant to the sight, and good for food; the tree of
life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good
and evil. ...Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field
which the LORD God had made. And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God
said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?

CHAPTER 2 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * luiram (noun): orchard, terraced garden, cultivated grove
    Ex: Edenli luiram akha.
  * thingphun (noun): standing tree, living botanical arbor
    Ex: Ringkaphavai thingphun.
  * chashak-kaphā (adj): cunning, subtle, crafty
    Ex: Phar china chashak-kaphā.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Agrarian botanical compound: 'luiram' (field/orchard land).
  - Negative interrogative particle: 'lei-macho?' (is it indeed so?).

CHAPTER 3: NOAH'S ARK & THE RAINBOW COVENANT (GENESIS 6-9)
Summary: The construction of the giant wooden vessel, the deluge, and the rainbow token o...
Tangkhul Text:
Genesis 6:14, 9:12-16: 'Nawui vang gopher thing eina kuphung kashang
akha salu; kuphungli shimsak salaga, kuphung kashikli kala kaphungli
dhar eina hulup-khalaplu.' ...Varivarana hang: 'Iwui kala nathumwui
ngachaili ... thot pupupwui vang I sakahai tui-ngashikwui theichitheina
hi: I Kazing-ngachamli Iwui Mayāngwon (Rainbow) sangkahaye; kala chiya
Iwui kala ngaleiwui ngachaili tui-ngashikwui theichithei sara. Kala I
mayāngwon theirarlaga mikumo kala ringkapha saphang pupupli thikhavai
kasham tuikhangahui masaralu.'
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Make thee an ark of gopher wood; rooms shalt thou make in the ark, and
shalt pitch it within and without with pitch. ...And God said, This is
the token of the covenant which I make between me and you and every
living creature that is with you, for perpetual generations: I do set my
bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me
and the earth. And I will remember my covenant, that the waters shall no
more become a flood to destroy all flesh.

CHAPTER 3 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * Mayāngwon (noun): rainbow, celestial arch of flowers/colors
    Ex: Kazing-ngachamli Mayāngwon.
  * tui-ngashik (noun): covenant, sacred treaty, binding pact
    Ex: Tui-ngashikwui theichithei.
  * tuikhangahui (noun): great flood, deluge, catastrophic inundation
    Ex: Kasham tuikhangahui.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Poetic cultural compound: 'Mayāngwon' (lit. bloom/arch of the skies).
  - Perpetual covenant marker: 'thot pupupwui vang' (for all generations).

CHAPTER 4: THE CALL OF ABRAHAM & THE COVENANT OF FAITH (GENESIS 12 & 15)
Summary: Abraham's departure from Ur and the promise of descendants numerous as the stars...
Tangkhul Text:
Genesis 12:1-3, 15:5: Prohona Abramli hang: 'Nawui ram, nawui shimanwui
ngalung, kala nawui avashim eina shoklaga, I chitheikhavai ramli valoo!
Kala I nawui eina ngashan kashang akha sarap sara, kala I nawui phāsan
milu sara... kala nawui ming eina ngaleiwui ngashan pupupna mathāsek-u
sara.' ...Ana Abramli kaphung eina yangsaklaga hang: 'Kazingli yanglu,
sirabingli kharep-shukralang khareplu!' Kala Ana hang: 'Nawui ngashan
chi hithada sara.' Kala Abramna Proholi chotkhangashit khuiye; kala
chiya awui mashung-kasa hoda hisangmi.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Now the LORD had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from
thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I will shew
thee: And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and
make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing: And in thee shall all
families of the earth be blessed. ...And he brought him forth abroad,
and said, Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to
number them: and he said unto him, So shall thy seed be. And he believed
in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.

CHAPTER 4 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:

    Ex: Nawui avashim eina.
  * sira (noun): star, celestial body

  * chotkhangashit (noun/verb): faith, to believe unwaveringly

Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:

CHAPTER 5: JOSEPH AND HIS BROTHERS IN EGYPT (GENESIS 37 & 45)
Summary: The coat of many colors, the descent into Egypt, and the reconciliation of Josep...
Tangkhul Text:
Genesis 37:3, 45:3-5: Israelna Josephli azao-azana tharili pamei
ngayeiye; kala ana awui vang kachon kakhangatei kaphā akha sakahaisai.
...Josephna awui uningnaobingli hang: 'I Joseph-sai! I-ava ring-leirang-
macho?' Kha uningnaobingna awui ngalemli ngachek-kashung thotlaga
ngahan-mashuk-mana. Kala Josephna hang: 'I ngalemli rarlaklu... nathumna
ili hili pashitsang-kaji vang ningkasham makasaloo: kasakei mikumowui
ringkaphavai huikhuikhavai Varivarana ili nathumwui mik-ngalemli
kashokahaisaye!'
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, because he was the
son of his old age: and he made him a coat of many colours. ...And
Joseph said unto his brethren, I am Joseph; doth my father yet live? And
his brethren could not answer him; for they were troubled at his
presence. And Joseph said unto his brethren, Come near to me, I pray
you... Now therefore be not grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that ye
sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life.

CHAPTER 5 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * uningnaobing (noun): brothers, sibling clan members
    Ex: Awui uningnaobingli hang.
  * pashitsang (verb): sold into bondage / bartered away
    Ex: Ili hili pashitsang.
  * ningkasham (noun): sorrow, inner anguish, bitter grief
    Ex: Ningkasham makasaloo.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Prohibitive emotional phrase: 'ningkasham makasaloo' (Do not make grief of heart).
  - Sibling collective plural: 'uningnaobing' (older and younger brothers).
