## BK-LIT-003 -- Preface & Archival Imprint
### Preface & Archival Documentation
PREFACE BY Y. K. SHIMRAY:
This volume constitutes a classic compendium of ancestral Tangkhul oral folktales (Khararchan),
mythological legends of village foundations, animal fables, and moral narratives.
Compiled to bridge the cultural heritage between hill and valley communities in Manipur,
it documents the wisdom, humor, and cosmic worldview of ancient Tangkhul storytellers.

## Chapter 1: The Legend of Hundung (Page 4)
### Chapter 1: The Legend of Hundung & The White Cow
CHAPTER 1: THE LEGEND OF HUNDUNG & THE WHITE COW
Summary: The founding legend of Hundung village guided by the sacred pregnant white cow.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Awo-ayiwui thotchanli: Seiva kachang akha leilaga apha kapha apamli vatuda seinao phanung tharanli Hundung khaung phokna.
According to ancestral tradition, the founders followed a sacred white cow across the central ridges.
Wherever the cow rested upon the gentle grassy plateau, there she gave birth to her calf,
and upon that auspicious ground the venerable village of Hundung (Hungpung) was established.

## Chapter 2: The Two Brothers (Page 6)
### Chapter 2: The Two Brothers & Migration to the Hills
CHAPTER 2: THE TWO BROTHERS & MIGRATION TO THE HILLS
Summary: The common ancestry of hill and valley peoples.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Chinaoni khani wui tui: kakhara a kaphungli pamda kakhanao a ngaleili vaipama.
The elder brother made his home upon the cool hill heights of Ukhrul, hunting and tilling terrace slopes,
while the younger brother cultivated the fertile plain, agreeing to send annual gifts of mountain honey and cotton cloth.

## Chapter 3: Animal Fables (Page 8)
### Chapter 3: Animal Fables: The Tiger and the Clever Toad
CHAPTER 3: ANIMAL FABLES: THE TIGER AND THE CLEVER TOAD
Summary: Wit overcomes brute strength in the forest.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Khawo eina khanei wui khararchan: Khawo khamakang ana ngashida leikha khanei them lak eina makhamatuina.
The mighty tiger boasted of his swiftness, but the tiny toad challenged him to a jumping race across the rocky stream,
outwitting the beast by clinging to his tail and claiming victory on the opposite bank.

## Chapter 4: The Story of Huimei (Page 10)
### Chapter 4: The Story of Huimei and Mountain Spirits
CHAPTER 4: THE STORY OF HUIMEI AND MOUNTAIN SPIRITS
Summary: Encounters with Kameo (forest spirits) upon misty ridges.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Huimei kaphungli valaga kameowui tui theiser; khangachei maleila shitsangda ungchan thotna.
Huimei journeyed through the primeval oak forest where spirits whisper among the moss-covered stones,
learning the sacred names of herbs and preserving ancient medical knowledge for his village.

## Chapter 5: Discovery of Rice & Salt (Page 12)
### Chapter 5: The Origin of Rice & The Discovery of Salt
CHAPTER 5: THE ORIGIN OF RICE & THE DISCOVERY OF SALT
Summary: How ancestors discovered saline springs at Marem and paddy seeds.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Machi eina ma machiwui shiyan: Marem kaphungli tara khamathei machikashak samphangna.
Paddy was granted to humanity by the creator Kasa Akhava, while saline springs at Marem and Kalhang
were discovered by hunters observing wild deer licking the mineral-rich cliffs.

## Chapter 6: Tales of Morung Bravery (Page 14)
### Chapter 6: Tales of Morung Bravery & Tricksters
CHAPTER 6: TALES OF MORUNG BRAVERY & TRICKSTERS
Summary: Feats of strength and humorous storytelling in the bachelor dormitory.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Longrawui yarsona ot kakhayei kasa eina lumlaoda pamshona.
Youths tested their endurance by wrestling, stone-putting, and competing in memory recitations,
preserving centuries of genealogical chants around the dying embers of the morung log.

## Chapter 7: Moral Allegories (Page 16)
### Chapter 7: Moral Allegories & Customary Wisdom
CHAPTER 7: MORAL ALLEGORIES & CUSTOMARY WISDOM
Summary: Traditional proverbs (Tuingashit) and customary jurisprudence.

TANGKHUL & ENGLISH TEXT:
Tangkhul tuingashit: 'Tui kakhayei eina tui khangaran phamei; kazeiram makazamana.'
True justice requires impartiality; an oath taken upon the cat's tooth or spearhead is sacred,
and truthfulness remains the crowning virtue of a Tangkhul warrior.

## Chapter 7: Proverbs (Page 17)
### Chapter 7 Traditional Tangkhul Proverbs
CLASSICAL TANGKHUL PROVERBS (TUINGASHIT):
  1. 'Pang eina kasa otna wuklungli karingna' (Labor with one's hands nourishes the soul).
  2. 'Khaung kaphā khangavawa wui tuiyana' (A good village reflects the wisdom of its council).
  3. 'Macha khararwui tui khangana kharingna' (Listening to elders prolongs one's days).
