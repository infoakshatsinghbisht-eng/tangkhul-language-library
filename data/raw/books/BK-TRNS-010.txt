Empowering indigenous communities requires accessible legal literacy in
their mother tongue. This primer renders fundamental constitutional
concepts—sovereignty, justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity—into
dignified Tangkhul legal prose, harmonizing constitutional democracy
with customary village court (*Hangva*) jurisprudence.

CHAPTER 1: THE PREAMBLE TO THE CONSTITUTION OF INDIA (SAMVIDHAN PREAMBLE)
Summary: Solemn translation of the Preamble into classical Tangkhul civic diction....
Tangkhul Text:
Samvidhan Preamble:
ITHUM INDIAWUI MIKUMO, India-li Sovereign, Socialist, Secular,
Democratic Republic akha sakhavai solemn ningkacham eina kasakakhui,
kala chiwui citizen pupupwui vang:
JUSTICE (Mashung-kasa): Social, economic, kala political;
LIBERTY (Khangachon / Mathat): Ningkhamathan, phasha-kashak,
chotkhangashit, kala worship;
EQUALITY (Khangayam): Thot-phā kala opportunity wui ngachaili;
FRATERNITY (Uningnao ngayeikasa): Mikumo akha-akhawui dignity kala
Nationwui unity kala integrity ngashankhavai;
ITHUI CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY-li November 26, 1949 zingsho eina Samvidhan
hi adopt salaga, enact salaga ithuwui aphasa thapupli miserie.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India
into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to
all its citizens: JUSTICE, social, economic and political; LIBERTY of
thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; EQUALITY of status and
of opportunity; and to promote among them all FRATERNITY assuring the
dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation; IN
OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do
HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.

CHAPTER 2: FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (ARTICLE 14 TO 32)
Summary: Rights to equality, freedom of speech, personal liberty, and constitutional reme...
Tangkhul Text:
Fundamental Rights (Hao Citizen Rights):
1. Article 14: Law-wui ngalemli mikumo pupup khangayam leisaye (Equality
before law).
2. Article 19: Tui hangkhavai, phanit sakhavai, kala union kasakhavai
mathat (Freedom of speech & assembly).
3. Article 21: Ringkapha kala personal liberty huikhuikhavai rights.
4. Article 25: Chotkhangashit kala reot-kasavai freedom.
5. Article 29-30: Minority language, script kala culture ngashankhavai
rights.
6. Article 32: Supreme Court kala High Court-li writ petition eina
rights huikhuikhavai remedies.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Overview of Part III of the Constitution: Equality before the law, six
freedoms of Article 19, protection of life and personal liberty under
Article 21, freedom of religion under Article 25, minority cultural and
linguistic protections under Articles 29-30, and the right to
constitutional remedies under Article 32.

CHAPTER 3: CUSTOMARY LAW, VILLAGE COURTS (HANGVA) & ARTICLE 371
Summary: Integration of indigenous tribal customary law, village councils, and constituti...
Tangkhul Text:
Hao Ngashan kala Constitutional Protection:
Tangkhul village pupupli traditional council hoda Hangva (Village Court)
leisaye. Awungshina headman hoda court-wui tui khak-kasaye. Article 371A
kala 371C-wui provisions eina tribal customary laws, land ownership
(ngalei khangayei), and civil justice-li constitutional autonomy
miserie. Hangvawui decisions customary law eina sashung-khalang, formal
law courtsna chichi respect salaga ngashane.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Tribal customary governance operates through the Hangva (Village Council
and Court) presided over by the Awungshi (village chief/headman).
Constitutional provisions including Article 371 safeguard indigenous
customary laws, land tenure systems, and traditional conflict resolution
from arbitrary legislative interference.
