PREFACE & TRANSLATION HISTORICAL CONTEXT
The translation of Aesop's Fables by the Tangkhul Literature Society
provided generation after generation of Tangkhul school children with
lively didactic stories. The translator ingeniously mapped Aesop's
animals to familiar Himalayan highland counterparts: the hare became
'ngachon', the tortoise 'shimhar', the wolf 'ngayen', and the fox
'sharu', preserving the universal moral truth of each fable.
Archival Catalog Reference: TLS-TKH-AESOP-1978

CHAPTER 1: SHIMHAR KALA NGACHON (THE TORTOISE AND THE HARE)
Summary: The boastful hare challenged by the slow but persevering tortoise in Tangkhul pr...
Tangkhul Text:
Khararchan 1: Thot akha ngachon kashap akha leisai. Ana kashikatha sha
pupupli 'I thada khangachon pamei malei!' hoda aphasa mathaikhangasang
leisai. Chitharan shimhar kacham akha rarlaga hang: 'Ngachon, na eina i
race khaksa-u!' Ngachonna mania: 'Na thada kachut-kachut zatnao china
ili khaksa-ra kaji?' Kha athum race phokserie. Ngachonna shongzali
khangachon thotlaga 'Shimhar kashapli kachut; i thingphun ngachingli
ngaphama-u' hoda pilakna. Kha shimharna kakhut machamla zatpingsanglaga,
goal line-li khare vashukrie! Moral (Tuitam): Ningkazang eina chotkasana
khangaraorap khuiye.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
A Hare was making fun of the Tortoise one day for being so slow. 'Do you
ever get anywhere?' he asked with a mocking laugh. 'Yes,' replied the
Tortoise, 'and I get there sooner than you think. Run with me and I will
prove it.' The Hare was much amused at the idea of running a race with
the Tortoise, but for the fun of the thing he agreed. The Hare was soon
far out of sight, and to make the Tortoise appear very ridiculous, he
lay down beside the course to take a nap until the Tortoise should catch
up. The Tortoise meanwhile kept going slowly but steadily, and, after a
time, passed the place where the Hare was sleeping. When the Hare awoke,
the Tortoise was across the finish line! Moral: Slow and steady wins the
race.

CHAPTER 2: SHARU KALA SAKALU (THE FOX AND THE GRAPES)
Summary: The cunning fox leaping for sweet grapes and declaring them sour when out of rea...
Tangkhul Text:
Khararchan 2: Sharu akha kachak kashaklaga ramkhangateili zatlakha,
thingphunli shantuk-kahai sakalu (grapes) theitserie. Sakalu chi thik-
thik kacha, chithei kaphā kala tui-ngapom kachuk theilaga sharu chi
shashik thotna. Ana sakalu khukhavai kaphungli thuksange; kha vashuk
mashuk-mana. Khani, kathum chuksa-ngasotlakha khikha phā mashuk-mana.
Chitharan sharuna awui makhayor mathatlaga hang: 'Khai! Sakalu hi
kakhui-kashasai! Ili kakhui thei shakkhavai ning maleimana!' hoda
valakna. Moral (Tuitam): Phā-mashuk-khalangli kashikahai hoda hangkasa
mi kachungsai.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
A famished Fox will tell you, if he can, of the sweet grapes that hung
from a vine high on a trellis. He longed to reach them, but they were
beyond his leap. He took a running start and jumped, but missed. Again
and again he tried after the tempting morsel, but at last had to give it
up. He walked off with his nose in the air, saying: 'I thought those
grapes were ripe, but I see now they are quite sour.' Moral: It is easy
to despise what you cannot get.

CHAPTER 3: AVAIKHA KACHAM NGALANAO KALA NGAYEN (THE BOY WHO CRIED WOLF)
Summary: The young shepherd crying wolf falsely and finding no one believes a liar even w...
Tangkhul Text:
Khararchan 3: Mayarnaowon akhana kaphungli avaikha (sheep) kashun
leisai. Ana shongfa khangayeili ngacham-kaza chiwui vang khanganoilaga
hohe: 'Ngayen! Ngayen rarlakna! Avaikha shaksangie!' Village-wui mikumo
thapup thingkot khuilaga khangachonli rarer; kha chithuili ngayen malei-
mana, mayarnaowon china kanganrilaga pamye. Zingkum khani hithada
saserie. Chitharan zingsho akha kashung-mashung ngayen rarlakna!
Mayarnaowon china lungkashung eina hohe: 'Ngayen rarlakna! Khangachon
milu!' Kha mikumo akhakhana mashursang-mana, 'A maningkachang tui
hangye' hoda leisai. Chitharan ngayenna avaikha pupupli shaksangserie.
Moral (Tuitam): Maningkachang mi kashung tui hangkhalangli khipana
machotsang-mana.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
There was once a young Shepherd Boy who tended his sheep at the foot of
a mountain near a dark forest. It was rather lonely for him, so he
thought upon a plan by which he could get a little company and some
excitement. He rushed down towards the village calling out 'Wolf, Wolf,'
and the villagers came out to help him. He laughed at them when they
found no wolf. This he did twice. At last, a real Wolf came out from the
forest. The boy cried in earnest terror, 'Wolf, Wolf, please come!' But
the villagers thought he was deceiving them again, and nobody came to
his aid. Moral: Nobody believes a liar, even when he tells the truth.

CHAPTER 4: SAMAYĀR KALA KHACHANGNAO (THE LION AND THE MOUSE)
Summary: The mighty lion sparing the tiny mouse, who later gnaws the hunter's ropes to fr...
Tangkhul Text:
Khararchan 4: Samayār (lion) kashang akha thingphun ngachingli
ngaphamsai. Khachangnao (mouse) kacham akhana awui mai-ngalungli
ngachonkatlakha, samayārna awui khut eina khuikahaisai. Khachangnaona
khamathān haikaphā salaga hang: 'Shiyankasang, ili huikhuilu! Thot akha
i nawui khangachon sarap sara!' Samayārna kanganrilaga ali
kashikahaiserie. Zingsho charakha ngaphalaga, hunterbingna samayārli rui
kashang eina thuksangie. Samayārna khongyui eina hu-hu kashoklakha,
khachangnaona rarlaga awui khani-eina rui chi kakor-khakserie! Samayār
chi ngatorie. Moral (Tuitam): Kakatnaobingna kashangbingli khangachon
sara kashap lei.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
A Lion was awakened by a Mouse running over his face. Rising in angry
wrath, he laid his paw upon him with intent to kill him. The Mouse
piteously entreated him: 'Spare me, O King! and I will surely repay your
kindness.' The Lion laughed and let him go. Some time after, the Lion
was caught in the net of the hunters. He roared with fury. The Mouse
recognized his roar, ran to the net, and gnawed the ropes with his teeth
until he set the king free. Moral: Little friends may prove great
friends.

CHAPTER 4 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * samayār (noun): lion, king of predators
    Ex: Samayār kashang.
  * khachangnao (noun): little mouse, highland rodent
    Ex: Khachangnao kacham.
  * rui (noun): rope, braided fiber trap cord
    Ex: Rui kakor-khak.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Tangkhul rodent gnawing verb: 'kakor-khak' (to sever completely by gnawing).
  - Diminutive suffix '-nao' marking smallness/humility (khachangnao = tiny mouse).
