PREFACE & TRANSLATION HISTORICAL CONTEXT
This anthology introduced Tangkhul secondary students to three
monumental gems of world narrative prose. The translations demonstrate
the narrative flexibility of modern standard Tangkhul, handling 19th-
century Russian spiritual realism, French bourgeois tragedy, and
American ironic compassion with equal brilliance.

CHAPTER 1: WHERE LOVE IS, GOD IS (KHANGANING KALA VARIVARA) -- LEO TOLSTOY
Summary: Martin Avdeitch the cobbler looking for Christ and finding Him in the freezing s...
Tangkhul Text:
Khararchan 1: Martin Avdeitch kaho shoemaker akhana khong-ngaching
shimli pamsai. Ana zingsho pupup Lairik Kathara hanglaga ningkazang
kazip-kasaye. Ngazing akha ana khongyui akhata kapeiye: 'Martin! Martin!
Yanglu zingsho kashokli I nawui ngalemli rara!' Zingkhamathān shoklakha,
Martinna window eina kashikathali yanglakha, Stepan kaho mayarnao kacham
kashikaza akha snow ngalungli ngachek-kashung pamsai theitserie.
Martinna ali shimsanglaga meikhangahui tharili tea kashak milaga
chotkhangashit tui hangmi. Chitharan shanaowon akha awui naonli kachon
maleilakha chirzat-lakha theilaga ali kachon kala paisa miye. Ngazing-
tharan Martinna theitserie: 'Awui mai eina Christana hung-kachitheisaye:
kasakei kachamnaobingli sakasashikahai ot pupup chi ili sakasashisaye!'
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
In a little basement room in a certain city lived Martin Avdeitch, a
cobbler. Every evening he read the Gospels until his heart burned within
him. One night he heard a voice calling him: 'Martin, look out into the
street tomorrow, for I will come.' Next day, while watching out the
window, he saw Stepanitch clearing snow, shivering in rags; he called
him in and gave him warm tea. Later he sheltered a poor mother with a
child wrapped in thin garments and gave her his cloak and money. At dusk
he realized: Christ had indeed visited him that day, for 'Inasmuch as ye
have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it
unto me.'

CHAPTER 2: THE NECKLACE (KHANGAPHEI WUI PHASANG) -- GUY DE MAUPASSANT
Summary: Mathilde Loisel borrowing the diamond necklace, losing it at the ball, and toili...
Tangkhul Text:
Khararchan 2: Mathilde Loisel kaho ngalashana kaphā akha pamsai; kha ana
kashang-phā saphā-kashangli pamei lungkakhui leisai. Ministry-wui grand
ball phanitli valakhavai ana awui ngalemwui Madame Forestier eina
diamond necklace (phasang) khuiye. Ball phanitli mathat-eina pheichak
salaga, shimli hung-kaphung tharan, theilu! Awui phasang chi ngashiman-
serie! Mathilde kala awui va-na ten years kuppup kashakashi ot salaga
36,000 francs kashunsangserie phasang kathara khuisangkhavai. Kum
thakharā ngaphalaga ana Madame Forestierli champsangie; chitharan
Forestierna hang: 'Oh, Mathilde! Iwui phasang chi paste (artificial)
sai! Awui value 500 francs kacham-sai!'
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Mathilde Loisel was a charming woman born into a family of clerks who
dreamed of wealth and luxury. Invited to a grand ball at the Ministry,
she borrowed a magnificent diamond necklace from her wealthy friend
Madame Forestier. She was the belle of the ball, but arriving home,
discovered to her horror that the necklace was lost. Rather than admit
the loss, she and her husband worked ten grueling years in squalor and
debt to purchase a replacement necklace for thirty-six thousand francs.
Ten years later, meeting Madame Forestier in the park, aged and haggard,
she learned the devastating truth: 'Oh my poor Mathilde! Mine was false!
It was worth at most five hundred francs!'

Vocabulary Entries:

    Ex: Diamond phasang.

    Ex: Ten years kashakashi ot.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:

  - French loanword handling: francs and ball phonetically adapted in Tangkhul narrative prose.

CHAPTER 3: THE GIFT OF THE MAGI (MAGIBINGWUI KHANGASHE) -- O. HENRY
Summary: Della selling her hair for a watch chain and Jim selling his watch for her hair ...
Tangkhul Text:
Khararchan 3: Della eina Jimna kacham shimli pamsai; Christmas tharili
Della-li dollar 1.87 kacham leisai. Awui va Jimli Christmas gift
mikhavai, ana awui kaphāmei sam (hair) pashitsanglaga Jimwui gold watch
wui platinum chain khuiye. Ngazing-tharan Jimna shimli zanglaga Della-
wui sam katat theilaga ngachekserie: kasakei Jimna awui gold watch
pashitsanglaga Della-wui sam kaphā kachikhavai tortoise-shell combs
khuisang-kahaisai! Athum khaniwui gift ngashiman thada theilashu,
athumna magibing thada kaphāmei khangayei eina kashang thotna.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
One dollar and eighty-seven cents was all Della had on Christmas Eve to
buy a present for her husband Jim. In sacrificial devotion, she sold her
glorious cascading brown hair for twenty dollars to purchase a platinum
fob chain for Jim's prized gold watch. When Jim returned home, he stared
at her shorn hair in stunned amazement: for he had sold his gold watch
to purchase the expensive set of tortoiseshell combs Della had long
coveted! Though their material treasures were made temporarily useless,
they were the wisest of all who give gifts--they were the Magi.

CHAPTER 3 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * khangashe (noun): sacrificial gift, offering of love
    Ex: Christmas khangashe.
  * sam (noun): head hair, long locks
    Ex: Della-wui sam kaphā.
  * magibing (proper noun): The Magi, Wise Men from the East
    Ex: Magibing thada.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Reciprocal sacrifice pattern: X sells A to buy for Y; Y sells B to buy for X.
  - Tangkhul term for mutual devotion: 'khangayei eina kashang'.
