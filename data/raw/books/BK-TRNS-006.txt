PREFACE & TRANSLATION HISTORICAL CONTEXT
John Bunyan's 1678 allegory was rendered into Tangkhul Naga in the early
1930s. The allegory resonated profoundly with Tangkhul storytelling
traditions: perilous mountain passes, giants, bogs of despondency, and
fortified spiritual gates. It became one of the most widely read prose
texts in Ukhrul and the surrounding hills.
Archival Catalog Reference: TLS-TKH-PILGRIM-1932

CHAPTER 1: THE BURDENED PILGRIM & DEPARTURE FROM THE CITY OF DESTRUCTION
Summary: Christian weeping over his heavy burden, reading the Book, and Evangelist pointi...
Tangkhul Text:
Chapter 1: I kachak ngaleili ramkhangateili yanglakha, kasham shillongli
mayarnaowon akha theitserie. Awui pakhonli phashan kashang akha
phunkahaye, kala awui khutli Lairik akha huikahaye. Ana Lairik chi
hanglaga ngachire, kala 'I khipa sara? I katha eina huikhuira?' hoda
chirkhangahok eina hohe. Chitharan Evangelist kaho mi akha awui ngalemli
rarlaga hang: 'Nawui mik-ngalemli khong kakat chi theimacho?'
Mayarnaowon china hang: 'Thei-mashuk-mana.' Evangelistna hang:
'Chitharan mangashan kashang chi theilu, kala chichi shurva-lu;
chitharan na khong chithuili zanglaga huikhuira!'
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
As I walked through the wilderness of this world, I lighted on a certain
place where was a den, and I laid me down in that place to sleep: and as
I slept, I dreamed a dream. I looked, and saw a man clothed with rags,
standing in a certain place, with his face from his own house, a book in
his hand, and a great burden upon his back. He wept and trembled; and
not being able longer to contain, he brake out with a lamentable cry,
saying, What shall I do? Then said Evangelist, pointing with his finger
over a very wide field, Do you see yonder wicket-gate? The man said, No.
Then said the other, Do you see yonder shining light? Keep that light in
your eye, and go up directly thereto: so shalt thou see the gate.

CHAPTER 1 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * kasham shillong (noun): City of Destruction, doomed town
    Ex: Kasham shillongli mayarnao.
  * pakhon (noun): back, shoulders carrying heavy load
    Ex: Awui pakhonli phashan.
  * khong kakat (noun): strait gate, narrow wicket entrance
    Ex: Khong kakat chi theilu.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:

  - Emotional burst expression: 'chirkhangahok eina' (with weeping lamentation).

CHAPTER 2: THE SLOUGH OF DESPOND & THE WICKET GATE
Summary: Christian sinks in the mire of despondency, is rescued by Help, and reaches the ...
Tangkhul Text:
Chapter 2: Athum khani shongfa eina zatlakha, khamathān ngalung
kashilashi kacham akhali tuisangserie; chiwui ming Despond Miram (Slough
of Despond) hose. Chitharan Christianna awui pakhon kashang chiwui vang
ngalungli ngashumsangie. Kha theilu, Help kaho mi akha rarlaga awui
khutli huitsangserie, kala hang: 'Khikha mangachek-alu, shongfa
mashungli rarlu!' Chiwui tharan Christianna khong kakatli vashuklaga
kakorrie; kala Goodwillna khong hungkahanglaga ali shimsangie.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
They drew near to a very miry slough, that was in the midst of the
plain; and they being heedless, did both fall suddenly into the bog. The
name of the slough was Despond. Here therefore they wallowed for a time,
being grievously bedaubed with dirt; and Christian, because of the
burden that was on his back, began to sink in the mire. Then came a man
to him whose name was Help, and said, Give me thine hand: so he gave him
his hand, and he drew him out, and set him upon sound ground. Then
Christian came up to the gate, and knocked, and Goodwill opened unto
him.

CHAPTER 2 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:

    Ex: Ngalungli ngashumsang.
  * kakorrie (verb): knocked persistently
    Ex: Khong kakatli kakorrie.

  - Sequential narrative suffix '-laga': 'vashuklaga kakorrie' (having arrived, he knocked).

CHAPTER 3: THE HILL DIFFICULTY & THE PALACE BEAUTIFUL
Summary: Climbing the steep precipice of Difficulty, resting in the arbor, and arriving a...
Tangkhul Text:
Chapter 3: Christianna Hill Difficulty kaho kaphung kashangli vashukrie.
Shongfa chinawui chishatna kaphung kathara thada vaye, kala ana kakhut
eina, khut eina kakhorlaga thuk-kashok-e. Kaphung wui ngalemli ana Shim
Kaphā (Palace Beautiful) theitserie; kala chithuili Prudence, Piety,
kala Charity kaho ngalanaobingna ali mathat-eina shimlaga kachak kaphā
shaksakserie, kala khangaraorapwui thingkot chitheitserie.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
I looked then after Christian, to see him go up the hill, where I
perceived he fell from running to going, and from going to clambering
upon his hands and his knees, because of the steepness of the place.
Then he came to the house called Beautiful; and the porter Watchful
called Discretion, Piety, Prudence, and Charity, who welcomed him in,
and gave him refreshment, and showed him the armory of celestial
weapons.

  * kakhorlaga (verb): clambering on hands and knees

CHAPTER 4: THE VALLEY OF HUMILIATION & CONFLICT WITH APOLLYON
Summary: Descent into the valley, dread confrontation with Apollyon, and victory with the...
Tangkhul Text:
Chapter 4: Christianna Longkhor Kakat (Valley of Humiliation) li
zangsangie. Chitharan theilu, Apollyon kaho kasham sha akha rarlaga awui
shongfali ngaphamsai. Awui aphasa meikashe katha, kala meikazat awui
khong eina shoke. Ana Christianli hang: 'Nawui ramli valoo, na iwui
mayarnao-sai!' Kha Christianna hang: 'I Kazingramwui Shiyankasangwui
mayarnao-sai!' Apollyonna meithei shuklaga Christianli haksangie; kha
Christianna awui sword eina Apollyonli kashukserie, kala Apollyonna
phana pholaga ngakaphirserie!
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Now, in this Valley of Humiliation, poor Christian was hard put to it;
for he had gone but a little way, before he espied a foul fiend coming
over the field to meet him; his name is Apollyon. He was clothed with
scales, and out of his belly came fire and smoke. Apollyon said, Thou
hast done this according to the proverb, Change a bad for a worse: but
there is no prince that will thus lightly lose his subjects, neither
will I as yet lose thee: return. Christian answered, I am the servant of
the King of princes! Then did Christian nimbly stretch out his hand for
his sword, and caught it, saying, Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy:
when I fall, I shall arise. And with that he gave him a deadly thrust,
and Apollyon spread forth his dragon's wings, and sped him away.

CHAPTER 5: VANITY FAIR & THE CELESTIAL CITY (JERUSALEM KATHARA)
Summary: Faithful's martyrdom at Vanity Fair, Christian reaching the River of Death, and ...
Tangkhul Text:
Chapter 5: Christian kala Faithfulna Vanity Fair kaho phanit kashangli
vashukrie, chithuili mikumona ngaleiwui saphā-kashang kazip-kasaye.
Faithfulna kashung tui hangkaji vang chithuili thiri; kha ana
kazingramwui chariot eina chithangathukserie. Christianna Hopeful eina
zanglaga River of Deathli vashukrie; kala kapei tui katek-tek ngakorlaga
athum kazingramwui Shillong Katharali vashukserie! Chitharan athumli
kachon kachak sangmi, kala shillong tharili trumpet khongrie: 'Rarlu,
ithui Shiyankasangwui mathatli zanglu!' Kala i khangayangli ngakhur-
rarlakna.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
Then I saw in my dream, that they went on, until they came into a fair,
called Vanity Fair, where all kinds of worldly wares are sold. Faithful
was condemned and put to death, but a chariot waited to take him through
the clouds into the Celestial City. Christian and Hopeful came to the
River without a bridge, and passing through the deep waters, they
arrived at the Mount Zion. The Shining Ones welcomed them: Enter ye into
the joy of your Lord. And all the bells in the city rang again for joy;
and lo, it was a dream, and I awoke.

CHAPTER 5 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * Vanity Fair (proper noun): Phanit Kashilashi (Fair of Empty Vanities)
    Ex: Vanity Fairli vashuk.
  * kachon kachak (noun): pure white robe, shining garment
    Ex: Kachon kachak sangmi.
  * ngakhur-rarlakna (verb): awoke from dream vision, opened eyes
    Ex: I khangayangli ngakhur.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Dream-vision awakening frame: 'kala i khangayangli ngakhur-rarlakna' (and lo, I awoke).
  - Contrast between 'ngaleiwui saphā' (worldly riches) and 'kazingramwui mathat' (heavenly joy).
