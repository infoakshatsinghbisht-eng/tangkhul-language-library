PREFACE & TRANSLATION HISTORICAL CONTEXT
The Gospel of Mark is characterized by rapidity of action and direct
vivid narration. Translated by William Pettigrew and published by BFBS
in 1909. In Tangkhul, frequent narrative connectives like 'chitharan'
(thereupon) and 'khamathān' (immediately) showcase vernacular verbal
sequencing.

CHAPTER 1: THE MINISTRY OF JOHN THE BAPTIST & BAPTISM OF JESUS (MARK 1)
Summary: The voice crying in the wilderness and the opening of the gospel narrative in Ta...
Tangkhul Text:
Mark 1:1-4: Jesu Christa, Varivarawui Naomayārwui khararchan kaphung:
Isaiah kaho tui-theikhanana kasa thada leiri: 'Theilu, Nawui mik-
ngalemli Iwui pao-phungnaoli kasho vaye, ana nawui shongfa ngalunghang
sara. Ramkashilashili khongyui akhana hohe: 'Prohowui shongfa kapanglu,
Awui lambili mashunglu.' Johnna ramkashilashili baptism kasami thotna,
kala yorshi ngashankahavai ningkaphuritwui baptism pao phongie.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God; As it is
written in the prophets, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face,
which shall prepare thy way before thee. The voice of one crying in the
wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make his paths straight.
John did baptize in the wilderness, and preach the baptism of repentance
for the remission of sins.

CHAPTER 2: THE STILLING OF THE STORM (MARK 4)
Summary: Miracle on the Sea of Galilee demonstrating authority over wind and waves in Tan...
Tangkhul Text:
Mark 4:35-41: Chi zingsho ngazing-tharan, Ana athumli hang: 'Ngashakazil
kakahara kachili ithum ngakorva-u.' Kala mikumo kachungli
kashikahailaga, A kuphungli leisai thada thukpingserie. Chitharan hu-hu
kaho huimayon kasham akhata rarer, kala tui-ngachaona kuphung tharili
ngashingye, chithada kuphung chi tui eina kashung thotna. Kala A kuphung
wui kashikli mikazak akhali luipatlaga ngaphamsai: kala athumna ali
pholaga hang: 'Tuitamkasana, ithum mathira chi Nawui ningli malei-
macho?' Kala A shoklaga huili kaphangserie, kala ngashakazilli hang:
'Maza-lu, ngacham-lu!' Chitharan huichi ngachaoran thotna, kala
chitharan ngakazil khamatha akhata shokserie.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
And the same day, when the even was come, he saith unto them, Let us
pass over unto the other side. And when they had sent away the
multitude, they took him even as he was in the ship. And there arose a
great storm of wind, and the waves beat into the ship, so that it was
now full. And he was in the hinder part of the ship, asleep on a pillow:
and they awake him, and say unto him, Master, carest thou not that we
perish? And he arose, and rebuked the wind, and said unto the sea,
Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm.

CHAPTER 3: THE FEEDING OF THE FIVE THOUSAND (MARK 6)
Summary: Multiplication of the five loaves and two fishes in Tangkhul hospitality idiom....
Tangkhul Text:
Mark 6:38-44: Ana athumli hang: 'Nathumli raruikatha kaphara lei?
Vayanglu.' Kala theilaga athumna hang: 'Phangā lei, kala ngachayei khani
lei.' Ana athumpup-li haikaphā salaga kacham eina ngashalungli pamkazip
milu hoda hangserie. Kala athum thakharā eina, phangā-magā eina
ngaburlaga pamserie. Kala Ana raruikatha phangā chi kala ngachayei khani
chi khuilaga, kazingli yanglaga Varivarali mathat-eina kashaklaga
raruikatha chi khakserie; kala mikumo tharili shunsangserie. Kala
athumpup shaksarlaga phasa kashung thotna.
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
He saith unto them, How many loaves have ye? go and see. And when they
knew, they say, Five, and two fishes. And he commanded them to make all
sit down by companies upon the green grass. And they sat down in ranks,
by hundreds, and by fifties. And when he had taken the five loaves and
the two fishes, he looked up to heaven, and blessed, and brake the
loaves, and gave them to his disciples to set before them. And they did
all eat, and were filled.

CHAPTER 3 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * raruikatha (noun): bread, unleavened loaf, baked staple
    Ex: Raruikatha phangā.
  * ngachayei (noun): fish, freshwater fish
    Ex: Ngachayei khani.
  * ngashalung (noun): green grass, pasture meadow
    Ex: Ngashalungli pamkazip.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Distributive numerical expressions: 'thakharā eina, phangā-magā eina' (by hundreds, by fifties).
  - Completive satiety marker: 'phasa kashung thotna' (their bodies became completely satisfied).

CHAPTER 4: THE GREATEST COMMANDMENT & THE WIDOW'S MITE (MARK 12)
Summary: Love for God and neighbor, alongside the sacrificial offering of the poor widow....
Tangkhul Text:
Mark 12:29-31, 42-44: Jesuna ngahanmi: 'Hingchithei kaphāmeina hi: O
Israel, kapeilu; Proho ithui Varivara, Proho akhachana! Kala nawui
lungkashung eina, nawui mangazing eina, nawui ning-kacham eina Proho
nawui Varivarali ngayeilu: hi hingchithei khare. Kala khakhane chi
hithada: 'Nawui aphasa thada nawui ngalemwui mikumoli ngayeilu.' ...Kala
ngalashang kacham akha rarlaga paisa khani haksangie. Jesuna hang:
'Ngalashang hi ana kachikathāna haksangmeiserie; kasakei a kacham thotli
awui ringkaphavai leikashap kuppup haksangserie.'
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
And Jesus answered him, The first of all the commandments is, Hear, O
Israel; The Lord our God is one Lord: And thou shalt love the Lord thy
God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind,
and with all thy strength: this is the first commandment. And the second
is like, namely this, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. ...And
there came a certain poor widow, and she threw in two mites. And Jesus
said, This poor widow hath cast more in, than all they which have cast
into the treasury: For all they did cast in of their abundance; but she
of her want did cast in all that she had, even all her living.

CHAPTER 4 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * lungkashung (noun): whole heart, sincere inner core
    Ex: Lungkashung eina.
  * ngalashang (noun): widow, bereaved woman
    Ex: Ngalashang kacham.
  * ringkaphavai (noun): livelihood, substance for living
    Ex: Awui ringkaphavai kuppup.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Instrumental postposition 'eina' emphasizing holistic totality ('with all thy heart').
  - Comparative superiority: 'haksangmeiserie' (cast in far more than all).

CHAPTER 5: THE RESURRECTION & FINAL COMMISSION (MARK 16)
Summary: The empty tomb at sunrise and the mandate to proclaim good tidings everywhere....
Tangkhul Text:
Mark 16:1-6: Sabbath ngaphalaga, Mary Magdalene, Jameswui ava Mary, kala
Salomena rarlaga Awui phasa kakhalaphavai meithei-chot khuisangserie.
Kala hoptawui zingsho khareli, zingkhamathān shoklakha lungkhorli
rarlakna. Kala theilu, lungphung kashang chi lunkhor khong eina
khangayot theiserie. Kala lungkhor tharili zanglaga kashang-phā chon
kachak kazang mayarnaowon akha pamsai theilaga athum ngachek-kashung
thotna. Kha ana athumli hang: 'Mangachek-alu: Nazarethwui Jesu,
thingkhamirangli kahom chi nathum khayore; A ngatekrarlaga thotsangie; A
hili maleira: theilu, A pili kahai pam hi!'
English Translation / Hindi Meaning:
And when the sabbath was past, Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of
James, and Salome, had bought sweet spices, that they might come and
anoint him. And very early in the morning the first day of the week,
they came unto the sepulchre at the rising of the sun. And they saw that
the stone was rolled away: for it was very great. And entering into the
sepulchre, they saw a young man sitting on the right side, clothed in a
long white garment; and they were affrighted. And he saith unto them, Be
not affrighted: Ye seek Jesus of Nazareth, which was crucified: he is
risen; he is not here: behold the place where they laid him.

CHAPTER 5 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Vocabulary Entries:
  * lungkhor (noun): rock tomb, stone cave sepulchre
    Ex: Lungkhorli rarlakna.
  * thingkhamirang (noun): cross, wooden crucifix, tree scaffold
    Ex: Thingkhamirangli kahom.
  * ngatekrarlaga (verb): having risen triumphantly, awakened from death
    Ex: A ngatekrarlaga thotsangie.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Tangkhul neologism for Cross: 'thing' (wood) + 'khamirang' (crossed/scaffold) = thingkhamirang.
  - Prohibitive imperative: 'Mangachek-alu' (Do not be frightened).
