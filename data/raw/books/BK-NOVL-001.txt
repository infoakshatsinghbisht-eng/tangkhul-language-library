PREFACE & HISTORICAL CONTEXT
Shimreishang kala Maitonphi stands as the undisputed crown jewel of
Tangkhul imaginative prose literature. Comparable in cultural stature to
Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet or Heer Ranjha of Punjab, this epic novel
recounts the star-crossed love of Shimreishang, a valiant youth of the
Zimikwo clan, and Maitonphi, the most graceful maiden of Phadang. It
vividly portrays 18th-century Tangkhul village life, jhum farming
rituals, village festivals, and the tragic conflict between individual
romantic passion and immutable tribal customary codes.
Archival Catalog Reference: TLS-TKH-NOVEL-SHIMREISHANG

CHAPTER 1: THE SPRING MEETING AT LUIRA FESTIVAL (LUIRA PHANITLI KHANGACHAM)
Summary: Shimreishang and Maitonphi meet during the Hao Pheichak circle dance in the flow...
Tangkhul Text:
Chapter 1: Zingyur atamli Luira Phanit shoklakha, Ukhrul ram kaphung-
longkhor kuppup mathāteiye. Shimreishang, Zimikwo shangwui mayarnao
kaphā, awui Haora chon eina pheichak shomye. Chitharan Maitonphi
Lungleng, Phadang ramwui ngalanao kachak-ngayei, Luirim kachon sanglaga
pheichak ngalungli ngaburleisai. Athum mik-ngalem khangacha tharan,
Shimreishangwui lungli wonkhamathā pomsangie. Ana Awui shai-laaring
honsangmi: 'Won kaphā, nali theilaga iwui lungkashungna nali
ngashankahuie!'
English Translation / Meaning:
When the spring seed-sowing festival (Luira Phanit) arrived, all the
hills and ravines of Ukhrul bloomed with celestial glory. Shimreishang,
the gallant son of the Zimikwo clan, adorned his shoulders with the
majestic Haora shawl to dance in the circle. There stood Maitonphi
Lungleng, the radiant maiden of Phadang, wrapped in her graceful Luirim
attire. When their gazes locked across the festive throng, a sacred
flower of love blossomed in Shimreishang's heart. He sang his poetic
lyric: 'O sweetest blossom, beholding thee, my whole heart surrenders in
eternal devotion!'

CHAPTER 1 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Key Vocabulary Entries:
  * Zingyur atam (noun): spring season, time of vernal blooms
    Ex: Zingyur atamli.
  * Haora (noun): traditional black and red striped ceremonial male shawl
    Ex: Haora chon.
  * wonkhamathā (noun): beautiful blossom of love, noble affection
    Ex: Wonkhamathā pomsang.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Seasonal temporal framing: 'Zingyur atamli... shoklakha' (When spring season arrived).
  - Poetic compounding: 'won' (flower) + 'khamathā' (beautiful) = flower of romance.

CHAPTER 2: THE SECRET VOW ON SHIRUI MOUNTAIN (SHIRUI KAPHUNGLI TUI-NGASHIK)
Summary: Ascending the mystical heights of Shirui Kashong, the lovers pledge eternal fait...
Tangkhul Text:
Chapter 2: Athum khani Shirui Kashong kaphung kashangli thukzat-serie.
Chithuili Shirui Lily won kaphā kachak eina ngasomleisai. Shimreishangna
Maitonphili Shirui Lily akha shitsanglaga awui samli hamsangmi.
Maitonphina mukthi kashik eina hang: 'Shimreishang, ithui shang khaniwui
ngachaili kashan lei; ithui avava-na ithuli ngaror-kashap sara macho?'
Shimreishangna awui khutli huitsanglaga tui-ngashik sakahaisai: 'Ngalei
hi ngapaoritsashu, iwui khangayei nawui vang thot pupup mathimara!'
English Translation / Meaning:
The two climbed the sacred crest of Mount Shirui, where the unique pink
Shirui Lilies bloomed in virgin purity. Shimreishang plucked a rare
blossom and lovingly placed it in Maitonphi's dark hair. With tears
glistening in her eyes, Maitonphi whispered: 'Shimreishang, an
unyielding clan barrier divides our families; will our parents ever
grant blessing to our union?' Grasping her hand firmly, Shimreishang
swore an inviolable oath: 'Though heaven and earth may vanish away, my
love for thee shall never perish throughout eternity!'

CHAPTER 3: CLAN FEUDS & THE FORCED BETROTHAL (SHANGWUI KASHAN KALA KAPHANKAHAI)
Summary: Inter-village clan hostilities erupt; Maitonphi's elders betroth her against her...
Tangkhul Text:
Chapter 3: Ukhrul kala Phadangwui ngachaili ramkashikhavai tui-ngapom
shokserie. Maitonphiwui avashimna ali Zimikwo shang eina khangayei
sakhavai kakhamrie. Chiwui tharan kashang awungshi akha rarlaga
Maitonphili marriage-wui vang bride-price (Man) mithukserie. Maitonphina
zingsho ngazing kachikhali chirkhangahok eina Shimreishangli
ningkhamathan leisai; kha customary law-wui khongyui kapei eina ana awui
va-vawui tui mathakakhui mashuk-mana. Ana Shimreishangli pao-phungnao
eina kashonmi: 'Iwui aphasa khangateili pashitsashu, iwui mangazing
nawuisai!'
English Translation / Meaning:
Bitter clan rivalries erupted between the territories of Ukhrul and
Phadang. Maitonphi's family strictly forbade any association with the
Zimikwo lineage. Soon, an influential chieftain arrived and paid a heavy
customary bride-price for Maitonphi's hand. Maitonphi wept day and night
in inconsolable anguish, yearning for Shimreishang; yet bound by the
iron edict of customary law, she could not disobey her elders. She sent
a secret message: 'Though my physical body be bartered away to another,
my soul belongeth solely unto thee!'

CHAPTER 3 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Key Vocabulary Entries:
  * Man (noun): traditional customary bride-price, marriage restitution
    Ex: Bride-price Man.
  * kakhamrie (verb): strictly prohibited, banned by taboo
    Ex: Khangayei kakhamrie.
  * chirkhangahok (noun): lamentation, agonizing weeping
    Ex: Chirkhangahok eina.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Customary law terminology: 'Man' (bride-price) and 'Kashan' (customary prohibition).
  - Concessive contrast: 'aphasa khangateili pashitsashu... mangazing nawuisai' (Though body be given, soul is thine).

CHAPTER 4: THE TRAGIC FAREWELL & ETERNAL UNION IN SPIRIT
Summary: Shimreishang returns to find Maitonphi has passed away of broken heart; their sp...
Tangkhul Text:
Chapter 4: Shimreishangna kaphung-longkhor kakhorlaga Phadangli ung-
kaphung tharan, theilu! Maitonphiwui shimli kathi-laa kakhong
theitserie. Maitonphina ningkasham kapei eina awui phasa mathirarlaga
Kazingramli valakna! Shimreishangna awui lungkhorli rarlaga lungkashung
eina chirpamsai: 'Maitonphi! Na mathida i katha eina ringkapamsara?'
Chitharan Shimreishangna awui thàng eina awui lungli kashukserie, kala
awui phasa Maitonphiwui ngalemli tuksangie. Ukhrulwui khararchanna
hangye: Athum khaniwui mangazing Shirui Kashongli wonkhamathā thada thot
pupup ngasomleisaye!
English Translation / Meaning:
When Shimreishang frantically traversed the gorges and reached Phadang,
alas! The haunting strains of the funeral lament echoed from Maitonphi's
home. Broken-hearted with grief, she had breathed her last. Collapsing
upon her freshly dug stone grave, Shimreishang wept in desperate agony:
'O Maitonphi! How can I endure this earthly life without thee?' Drawing
his ancestral dagger, he pierced his own heart and fell beside his
beloved. And the ancient legends of the Tangkhul recount: Their spirits
transformed into immortal Shirui lilies, blooming together in
inseparable embrace upon the mountaintop throughout all eternity.

CHAPTER 4 -- VOCABULARY & GRAMMAR
Key Vocabulary Entries:
  * kathi-laa (noun): funeral song, death lament, requiem dirge
    Ex: Kathi-laa kakhong.
  * thàng (noun): warrior's dagger, machete, ancestral dao
    Ex: Awui thàng eina.
  * ngasomleisaye (verb): blooming together in eternal harmony
    Ex: Thot pupup ngasomleisaye.
Grammar Notes & Idiomatic Usage:
  - Funeral lament compound: 'kathi' (death) + 'laa' (song) = dirge.
  - Immortal transformation narrative motif: physical death giving way to eternal floral rebirth.
