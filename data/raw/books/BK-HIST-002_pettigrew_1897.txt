## Tangkhul Primer and Catechism -- Preface & Imprint
### Preface & Archival Information
Tangkhul Primer and Catechism (Ukhrul, 1897) compiled by Rev. William
Pettigrew. This historic work represents the very first printed book in the
Tangkhul Naga language using the Roman alphabet, laying the foundations of
modern Tangkhul literacy.

## Chapter 1: Orthography, Vowels & Consonantal Phonetics (Page 4)
### Chapter 1: Orthography, Vowels & Consonantal Phonetics
CHAPTER 1: ORTHOGRAPHY, VOWELS & CONSONANTAL PHONETICS
Summary: The Roman alphabet system adapted for Tangkhul, macron markings, and glottal accents.

TANGKHUL READING TEXT:
Tangkhul Naga tuihi Roman script eina kapi shiyan khare. Vowels ā, ē, ī,
ō, ū hi khangashan kachung lei. Kakhalat kashak: 'a' kachihi khangarom
tui, 'ā' kachihi khamashung kashak. Glottal stop (') hi tuikashak
ngachaili shiyan kashakna.

ENGLISH TRANSLATION:
The Tangkhul language was reduced to writing using the Roman alphabet.
Vowel quantity is distinguished with macrons. Short vowels contrast with
long vowels. The glottal stop (') marks vital tonal and grammatical
separations in the Ukhrul dialect.

## Chapter 2: Noun Morphology, Gender & Case Suffixes (Page 6)
### Chapter 2: Noun Morphology, Gender & Case Suffixes
CHAPTER 2: NOUN MORPHOLOGY, GENDER & CASE SUFFIXES
Summary: Nominative, Agentive (-na), Accusative (-li), Genitive (-wui), Ablative (-eina).

TANGKHUL READING TEXT:
Tuiwon kasak shiyan: Agentive -na, Dative -li, Possessive -wui,
Instrumental -eina. Shimwui ava (master of house), mayarnaona ot sai
(the boy does work). Gender: mayār (masculine), va (feminine).

ENGLISH TRANSLATION:
Declension of nouns follows agglutinative postpositions. Agentive marker
-na identifies the transitive subject. Genitive -wui indicates
relationship, origin, and possession. Grammatical gender does not exist;
biological sex is indicated by suffixal markers.

## Chapter 3: Adjectives, Degrees of Comparison & Numerals (Page 8)
### Chapter 3: Adjectives, Degrees of Comparison & Numerals
CHAPTER 3: ADJECTIVES, DEGREES OF COMPARISON & NUMERALS
Summary: Prefix ka-, superlative pamei, and complete numeral tables from 1 to 100.

TANGKHUL READING TEXT:
Kaphā (good), kaphāmei (better), pamei (best). Kasau (tall), kazing
(deep). Numbers: akha (1), khani (2), kathum (3), mati (4), phangā (5),
tharuk (6), shini (7), chishat (8), chiko (9), thakharā (10), magā khani
(20), shā akha (100).

ENGLISH TRANSLATION:
Adjectives usually follow their governing nouns: e.g. mi kaphā (a good
man). Comparative suffix -mei; superlative pamei. Decimals: 10 is
thakharā or magā, 20 is magā khani, 100 is shā akha.

## Chapter 4: Pronouns: Personal, Demonstrative & Interrogative (Page 10)
### Chapter 4: Pronouns: Personal, Demonstrative & Interrogative
CHAPTER 4: PRONOUNS: PERSONAL, DEMONSTRATIVE & INTERROGATIVE
Summary: Pronoun declensions, inclusive vs exclusive first person, and demonstratives hi/chi.

TANGKHUL READING TEXT:
I (I), na (thou), a (he/she/it). Ithum (we), nathum (you all), athum
(they). Hi (this), chi (that). Khi (what), khipa (who), khishiwui
(whose), kachi (which). Iwui shimli unglo (come to my house).

ENGLISH TRANSLATION:
Personal pronouns take the same case suffixes as nouns: ina, ili, iwui.
Demonstrative pronouns hi (proximal: this) and chi (distal: that).
Interrogative pronouns: khipa (who), khi (what), kachi (which).

## Chapter 5: The Verbal System: Tenses, Moods & Causatives (Page 12)
### Chapter 5: The Verbal System: Tenses, Moods & Causatives
CHAPTER 5: THE VERBAL SYSTEM: TENSES, MOODS & CAUSATIVES
Summary: Conjugations, aspectual particles, auxiliaries lei and sa, and passive/causative morphemes.

TANGKHUL READING TEXT:
Present: i sai (I make/do). Past: i sada / i saser (I made/did). Future:
i sara (I will make/do). Subjunctive: a va-akha (if he goes). Causative:
i ali sa-sak-ei (I cause him to do). Auxiliary: lei (to exist, to have),
sa (to do, to be).

ENGLISH TRANSLATION:
Verbal morphology is rich and precise: tense inflections, conditional
suffixes (-akha), and causative markers (-sak). Existential auxiliary
'lei' expresses location and possession.

## Chapter 6: Conversational Phrases & Everyday Village Life (Page 14)
### Chapter 6: Conversational Phrases & Everyday Village Life
CHAPTER 6: CONVERSATIONAL PHRASES & EVERYDAY VILLAGE LIFE
Summary: Phrases for greeting, agricultural work, village council affairs, and family relations.

TANGKHUL READING TEXT:
Khangachan: 'Na katha lei?' -- 'I phaida lei.' 'Nawui ming khi hoyno?'
-- 'Iwui ming Somi hoyei.' 'Lui kasak tharan zingshorli kaphung
ngashitda ot kasa phamei.' 'Iram Ukhrulli won kapha wonri.'

ENGLISH TRANSLATION:
Conversational exchanges: 'How are you?' -- 'I am well.' 'What is your
name?' -- 'My name is Somi.' 'During terrace cultivation, working
together on the eastern hills is best.' 'In our Ukhrul homeland, lovely
blooms flourish.'
