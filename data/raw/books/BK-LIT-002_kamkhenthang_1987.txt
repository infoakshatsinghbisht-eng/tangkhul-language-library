## Chapter 1: Invocations & Spring Songs (Page 4)
### Chapter 1: Invocations & Spring Songs (Zingkum Hao Kaphok)
CHAPTER 1: INVOCATIONS & SPRING SONGS
Hokharai (Tribal Invocation) & Zingkum Hao Kaphok Laa (Song of Spring)

TANGKHUL TEXT:
AE-HE! Phamei yameisa HE... HE. O--O-HU-O! Ilongnao laa sa usa, Kashishoklu, kaphazanglu--O--Hui-O-hui!
O, kazingkum ura, o, kazingkum ura, o, hungnaovayo luithuimihaolu;
Mamanaomara, hungnaovayo luithuimashunga; Sampheirokna riri rakhonga, koktui vana zingkum phungraya!

ENGLISH RENDERING:
Hokharai invocation: May the better be ours! Let us go for singing, may evil depart and divine blessing enter!
O, the spring is started! Your highness, sow the seeds for the year; royal venture is just and timely;
The Sampheirok sings melodiously, and the cuckoo heralds the arrival of the bountiful new year!

## Chapter 2: Tarung Carrying Songs (Page 6)
### Chapter 2: Tarung Carrying & Monument Construction Songs
CHAPTER 2: TARUNG CARRYING & MONUMENT CONSTRUCTION SONGS
Song 2: Thingreikhangava Laa  |  Song 25: Ransak Laa

TANGKHUL TEXT:
O, yarso thingrei vakhang, thingpitang tak ungrall yaronnao, thingreiva ungreilu!
Seichang kharaei hangmi phalungra, phanora vashatta chaklen meikan naona kacheka, lumlao sakda vakhuiphalungra.
O, Kashongta phung-o, maranla saya; ilangkaso machilo, I wui luiyi kaya lhuiito, I wui chumrei hjorphiya!

ENGLISH RENDERING:
O young men, lift the monumental tree! The twigs bend, come swiftly with sturdy creepers;
The sacrificial mithun is provided, the augury bird Chaklen chirped favorably; let us carry home the Tarung!
From humble beginnings I built this house and pulled this stone; my terrace fields are fertile and granary full!

## Chapter 3: Ceremonial Dance Songs (Page 8)
### Chapter 3: Ceremonial Dance & Hornbill Movements (Pheishon Laa)
CHAPTER 3: CEREMONIAL DANCE & HORNBILL MOVEMENTS (PHEISHON LAA)
Song 3: Pheishon Laa (Dancing Song)

TANGKHUL TEXT:
O, iram hongreili, Mafathing kasui kharipna; Ngachamching arang shangkaya, arangchi khili kachanlo?
Mafathei channa, arangkho arangkhon ngashing leishiya; Thuingashing, kashung shanghaowa.

ENGLISH RENDERING:
In our thick ancestral forests, the stately Mafathing (Uningthou) trees abound;
Upon their broad boughs perch the majestic hornbills; why have they flocked in great numbers?
For the sweet Mafa fruits they have gathered; with rhythmic wingbeats they take flight towards Shirui Peak!

## Chapter 4: Maiden Dances & Youth Festivals (Page 10)
### Chapter 4: Maiden Dances & Youth Festivals (Yarra Laa)
CHAPTER 4: MAIDEN DANCES & YOUTH FESTIVALS (YARRA LAA)
Song 4: Laa Khanganui  |  Song 5: Yarra Laa

TANGKHUL TEXT:
Kazingkum huimailo aria, shivanaoli khina sale? Kha shingnai rao vasaya, he ring khaya zak matheiya.
Kongrali theo theo, ilongnaova khithing kaphato? Ilongnaova longthing kaphana, meishum kapamnao zangyo!

ENGLISH RENDERING:
May the years roll gently on! The maidens perform the sacred dance of purity under the winter sun.
What cheerful sounds echo along the stream? The youth gather firewood for the communal morung feast!

## Chapter 5: Agriculture & Pastoral Songs (Page 12)
### Chapter 5: Agriculture, Terrace Labor & Cowherd Songs
CHAPTER 5: AGRICULTURE, TERRACE LABOR & COWHERD SONGS
Song 7: Luisom Laa  |  Song 15: Sei Hom Laa

TANGKHUL TEXT:
O, I siluisa, I siluisa khinashai? I makreira khina mang, makreira sangralu; Shama maran saluira.
Ava khaho malei, aya kaho malei, seihom langphaizaya; Shok reo rangpamshona, silui shur khani chi kachui laga yaronnao chara horlama.

ENGLISH RENDERING:
Who will eat my rich buffalo meat, who will drink my aged rice beer? Pour freely for the terrace workers!
No father calls my name, no mother welcomes me home; alone upon the grassy slopes I herd the grazing buffalos,
Tending the herds from dawn till dusk while bitter tears roll down my sunburnt face.

## Chapter 6: Martial Ballads (Page 14)
### Chapter 6: Martial Ballads & Head-hunting Epics (Ral Laa)
CHAPTER 6: MARTIAL BALLADS & HEAD-HUNTING EPICS (RAL LAA)
Song 13: Ral Laa (War Song)

TANGKHUL TEXT:
O, atung shangla rai sakashiyavo, Lunghar yaron ni chiya! Azing takha rai sakashiyavo, Ringui yaron ni chiya!
Shangching Shangshak na ili khachitna chiya, shimin kayang ra mahon shok! Kharing sing singa, ili mangakatshelu!

ENGLISH RENDERING:
Behold in the northern crags the gallant knights of Lunghar stand ready in battle array!
In the southern ranges the brave youths of Ringui defend the mountain frontiers;
They fear me as the tiger of the forest; challenge me not, for our war cry shakes the valleys!

## Chapter 7: Traditional Romances (Page 16)
### Chapter 7: Traditional Love Romances (Shimreishang & Maitonphi)
CHAPTER 7: TRADITIONAL LOVE ROMANCES (SHIMREISHANG & MAITONPHI)
Song 19: Leishat Laa (Traditional Love Song)

TANGKHUL TEXT:
Avayo avayo, makhamalu, Maitonphili rasha saka; sakhangaiye Maitonphi kharam thuihaowa.
Laishikhavai chi lunamlizang ngaroksa; Coi marongthei reola khuisingda lung ringa.
Ameishang Ameishang, Ira kachangli thuiulu; Maitonphi, pangthui haosa, ini lungchan malaihaosa.

ENGLISH RENDERING:
Mothers and elders, do not forbid! For sweet Maitonphi alone does my heart beat in longing;
Though adverse winds separate us in this earthly life, our love remains rooted like the ancient pine tree.
Beloved Ameishang, farewell on this mortal shore; in the eternal meadows beyond, we shall part no more.

## Chapter 7: Literary Romance Analysis (Page 17)
### Chapter 7 The Epic of Shimreishang & Maitonphi
THE TRAGIC ROMANCE IN TANGKHUL LITERATURE:
The story of Shimreishang and Maitonphi represents the quintessential Tangkhul classical romance.
Composed of hundreds of antiphonal couplets, it explores the themes of unyielding loyalty,
social customs, clan expectations, and the spiritual conviction that authentic love survives death.

## Chapter 8: Weaving Songs & Festivals (Page 19)
### Chapter 8: Weaving Songs, Festive Banquets & Bedtime Chants
CHAPTER 8: WEAVING SONGS, FESTIVE BANQUETS & BEDTIME CHANTS
Song 20: Themsak Laa (Weaving Song)  |  Song 26: Pet Laa (Bed Time Song)

TANGKHUL TEXT:
O Tangkhul ngalanao yanglu yanglu, sak kasa kathemma themma; Haora chi yaron kachonna, Phangyai rah Tangkhul kashanna!
Phungcham vana ratha apamva rarona, phanishida tarikhana vaotun; Nali ungthuilo, sami ngachiya, i ram kashamshiraro.

ENGLISH RENDERING:
Look at the skilled Tangkhul maidens weaving their traditional cloths with masterly precision!
The black-and-red Haora shawl is for brave young men, and the Phangyai skirt for graceful women.
Evening falls across the high ridges of Phungcham; put aside the day's toil and rest peacefully in sleep.
