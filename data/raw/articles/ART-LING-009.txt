TITLE: Tangkhul Kinship Terminology, Consanguinity, and Lineage Structure: An Ethnolinguistic Model
AUTHORS: Prof. M. S. Thirumalai, Dr. Kapesa Khayi
JOURNAL / VENUE: Language in India (ISSN 1930-2940) (2014)
SUBJECT: Ethnolinguistics & Kinship Anthropology
KEYWORDS: Kinship, Omaha Type, Shang, Consanguinity, Ethnolinguistics, Thirumalai

ABSTRACT:
Kinship terminologies embody the social structural logic and reciprocal obligations of human societies. This paper presents the first exhaustive ethnolinguistic classification of Tangkhul kinship terminology, encompassing 68 consanguineal and affinal terms. We demonstrate that Tangkhul exhibits a bifurcate merging system of the Omaha subtype, reflecting strict patrilineal clan organization (Shang) and asymmetrical cross-cousin marriage rules.

SECTION: 1. Bifurcate Merging & Patrilineal Alignment
In Tangkhul, the father's brother is addressed by the same root as father (Ava-kakhano for father's younger brother, Ava-kakhara for father's elder brother), whereas the mother's brother is distinguished as Mawa/Kuku. Similarly, parallel cousins are equated with biological siblings (Uningnao), whereas cross-cousins fall into distinct terminological classes linked to marriageability.
Linguistic Data:
  * Ava : father
    Notes: Primary consanguineal male ancestor.
  * Ava-kakhano : father's younger brother
    Notes: Bifurcate merging parallel uncle.
  * Mawa / Kuku : mother's brother (maternal uncle)
    Notes: Affinal alliance partner.

SECTION: 2. Sibling Terminology & Seniority Distinctions
Seniority relative to the speaker is strictly encoded in sibling terms. Elder brother is achon/ayei, younger brother is atat/akhanao, elder sister is va-kakhara, and younger sister is va-kakhano. The collective compound uningnao encompasses all sibling kin within the Shang lineage.
Linguistic Data:
  * ayei : elder brother
    Notes: Senior male sibling.
  * akhanao : younger sibling (male or female)
    Notes: Junior sibling term.
  * uningnao : lineage siblings / clan brethren
    Notes: Collective kinship noun.

SECTION: 3. Affinal Terminologies & Asymmetrical Alliances
Affinal terms reflect mother's brother's daughter (MBD) preferential alliance. The wife's clan is honored as Mayāng-shang (life-giving in-laws), commanding ceremonial gifts of meat and woven shawls at all lifecycle rituals.
Linguistic Data:
  * Mayāng-shang : wife-giving affinal clan lineage
    Notes: Ceremonial in-law alliance.
