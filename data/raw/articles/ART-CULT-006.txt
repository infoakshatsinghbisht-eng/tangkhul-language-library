TITLE: Tangkhul Weaving and Textile Semiotics: Cultural Codes of Haora, Luirim, and Kashan
AUTHORS: Dr. Ninghorla Zimik, Prof. M. S. Thirumalai
JOURNAL / VENUE: Textile Anthropology & Cultural Studies Review (2016)
SUBJECT: Textile Anthropology, Weaving Semiotics & Material Culture
KEYWORDS: Weaving, Haora, Luirim, Kashan, Loin Loom, Textile Semiotics, Zimik

ABSTRACT:
Traditional handloom textiles constitute one of the most sophisticated visual languages and identity markers in Naga civilization. This paper analyzes the technology, color symbolism, and semiotic coding of traditional Tangkhul loin-loom (Porkapong) weaving. We examine the structural design, stripe frequencies, and ritual privileges associated with the male warrior shawl (Haora), the elite ceremonial wrap (Luirim), and the female wrap-around skirt (Kashan / Phingkham), demonstrating how textile motifs encode social status, clan affiliation, and cosmological harmony.

SECTION: 1. Loom Technology: The Traditional Loin Loom (Porkapong)
Tangkhul weaving is executed exclusively by women on the indigenous backstrap or loin loom (Porkapong / Khashan-thing). The loom consists of lightweight bamboo and polished timber rods (Tingkhor, Saphur, and Machor) tensioned by the weaver's own body weight. This portable loom permits exceptionally dense warp-faced weaves capable of withstanding decades of highland wear.
Linguistic Data:
  * Porkapong : traditional indigenous backstrap loin loom
  * Tingkhor : bamboo warp-beam rod
  * Machor : polished timber batten / beater sword

SECTION: 2. Color Semiotics and Structural Motifs: Haora & Luirim
Color palettes are drawn from native natural dyes: Meithai-ra (wild madder) for deep crimson red, Kharong-thing (indigo leaves) for deep raven black, and unbleached cotton for virgin white. The Haora shawl features broad black and white longitudinal bands framed by red selvedges, symbolizing bravery, purity, and warrior defense. The Luirim shawl, featuring intricate geometric diamond motifs (Kongra won), was historically reserved for village royalty and chiefs.
Linguistic Data:
  * Haora : traditional black and red striped male shawl
    Notes: Symbol of masculine dignity and defense.
  * Luirim : aristocratic ceremonial wrap with diamond motifs
    Notes: Reserved for chiefs and feast-givers.
  * Kongra won : diamond river-ripple geometric textile motif
    Notes: Aquatic cosmological emblem.

SECTION: 3. Female Attire: Kashan and Phingkham
The Tangkhul woman's wrap-around skirt (Kashan) serves as an overt visual manifesto of lineage identity. The Phingkham kashan, adorned with bold scarlet and black stripes, is worn during sacred festivals, whereas the Seichang kashan features delicate parallel lines worn during daily farm labor.
Linguistic Data:
  * Phingkham kashan : sacred festive female wrap-around skirt
    Notes: Formal ceremonial attire.
  * Seichang kashan : everyday agrarian working wrap skirt
    Notes: Practical agricultural textile.
