TITLE: Tangkhul Naga and Comparative Tibeto-Burman
AUTHORS: Prof. James A. Matisoff
JOURNAL / VENUE: Sino-Tibetan Etymological Dictionary and Thesaurus (STEDT) Monograph (1972)
SUBJECT: Comparative Sino-Tibetan Linguistics
KEYWORDS: STEDT, Matisoff, Proto-Tibeto-Burman, Cognates, Prefixation, Etymology

ABSTRACT:
This foundational paper analyzes the historical phonology and lexical morphology of Tangkhul Naga in the context of Proto-Tibeto-Burman (PTB) reconstruction. We examine over 120 basic vocabulary items from Pettigrew (1918) and Bhat (1969), identifying regular sound laws linking Tangkhul velar prefixes (kə-), nasal prefixes (mə-), and sibilant causatives (sə-) to pan-Sino-Tibetan morphosyntactic patterns. Tangkhul provides indispensable evidence for the antiquity of Tibeto-Burman sesquisyllabicity and nominalizing prefixation.

SECTION: 1. The Crucial Role of Tangkhul in PTB Reconstruction
Tangkhul Naga occupies a uniquely illuminating position in Tibeto-Burman comparative linguistics. While spoken in relative geographical proximity to the Meithei (Manipuri) kingdom and the Kuki-Chin hills, its grammatical structure preserves ancient prefixal paradigms that have eroded in more isolating languages of Southeast Asia. Rev. William Pettigrew's 1918 grammar and dictionary provided early linguists with their first systematic view of this archaic system.
Linguistic Data:
  * kathei : to see / perception
    Notes: PTB *m-ti-k; prefix ka- preserves nominalized state.
  * kashak : to eat / food
    Notes: PTB *dzya; regular correspondence with Written Tibetan za-ba.
  * kathi : to die / death
    Notes: PTB *s-təy; pan-Tibeto-Burman root found from Burma to Himalayas.

SECTION: 2. Prefixal Morphology and Derivational Functions
In Tangkhul, verbal roots rarely occur bare in citation form. Instead, they carry the ubiquitous prefix ka-, which functions simultaneously as an infinitive marker, action nominalizer, and adjectivalizer (e.g., pha 'be good' -> kaphā 'good'). Negative clauses replace this with the discontinuous morpheme ma-...-mana. Causatives are derived through suffixal -sak or archaic stem-initial aspiration, directly parallel to the Tibetan s- causative prefix.
Linguistic Data:
  * kaphā (good) / makaphā-mana (not good) : Affirmative vs Negative
    Notes: Discontinuous negation frame.
  * sasak (cause to do) eina kasa (to do) : Causative derivation
    Notes: Causative auxiliary affixation.

SECTION: 3. Tangkhul Etymological Cognates in STEDT
In the Sino-Tibetan Etymological Dictionary and Thesaurus (STEDT), Tangkhul cognates verify roots for anatomical terms, celestial phenomena, and agricultural implements. The preservation of distinction between initial velars and labiovelars corroborates reconstructions in Jingpho, Garo, and Old Chinese.
Linguistic Data:
  * mik : eye
    Notes: PTB *s-myak; identical coda stop with Burmese myak.
  * kakhut : foot, leg
    Notes: PTB *r-kay; velar stop preservation.
  * kho / khong : stream, ditch
    Notes: PTB *k-l(w)aŋ.
